Why I chose this model

I chose vosk-model-small-en-us-0.15, a lightweight US-English speech recognition model used with the Vosk toolkit. Vosk is designed for offline speech recognition and offers a streaming API, so it can show tentative words while you are speaking. The model provider describes this small model as suitable for lightweight devices such as Android devices and Raspberry Pi. Vosk overview · Model list

For JARVIS, I selected it as a first test because your PC has limited RAM, and our immediate goal is to see English text while you speak without sending your audio to a cloud service. This is a practical starting choice, not proof that it is the most accurate model for your voice.

How it works

1 · Listen
Read the microphone in roughly 0.2-second blocks.
2 · Prepare
Convert audio to 16 kHz for the recognizer.
3 · Recognize
Vosk continuously updates its hypothesis locally.
4 · Display
Show partial text, then final text after a pause.
Partial text can change. It is a provisional guess, not a confirmed instruction. JARVIS does not send it to Jev or use it to open apps yet.

What you should know

FeatureMeaning in this version
LanguageEnglish, using a US-English model. Other accents may work differently and need testing; no assumption about your accent.
Model sizeThe provider lists the compressed model at about 40 MB. The downloaded and extracted sizes on this PC are reported below.
Internet and privacyThe model must be downloaded once. Normal recognition uses the local model; the app code does not send or save microphone audio or transcripts. Displayed text, OS memory management, and other software are outside that guarantee.
LicenseVosk lists this model under Apache 2.0.
AccuracyThe provider reports 9.85% word error rate on LibriSpeech test-clean and 10.38% on TED-LIUM. These are their dataset results, not measurements of your voice or JARVIS commands.
LimitsNames such as “Chrome” and “Spotify,” background noise, fast speech, and different accents may cause errors. Partial results are unstable, and punctuation/capitalization may not be included.

Cloud speech recognitionAnother possible approach, but it requires network access and may involve audio transfer and usage fees. It does not meet the current local-first test goal.

We should decide based on the same spoken examples, not model names alone.

Test results on this PC

The official download is 39.3 MiB compressed and 67.6 MiB extracted. In one quick test, loading the model took 1.28 seconds; the process used 159 MiB of resident memory after loading and 238 MiB after recognition. These are snapshots, not peak memory.

We fed a 2.39-second Windows synthetic voice sample in 0.2-second blocks. It said “Open Google Chrome please” and the final result was “open google chrome please”. The first partial result appeared after 0.8 seconds of audio had been supplied; total decoding took 1.31 seconds. This sample was fed as fast as possible, not played in real time, so these numbers are not a measurement of end-to-end microphone latency.

Still to test: your real voice and microphone, your preferred speaking speed, background noise, real-time delay, and accuracy across a range of commands. A synthetic voice test is only a basic functional check.

Try it

  1. Open Start_English_Voice_Test.cmd and wait for the model to load.
  2. Say something like “Open Google Chrome” or “Open Spotify.” Watch the Partial and Final lines.
  3. Press q to stop. Share what you said and what appeared so we can improve it.

The current prototype only displays text; it does not open apps. To test the previously installed Japanese model from PowerShell, run .\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -X utf8 .\voice_live.py --language ja.
